## [Verisign Terminates All Existing .name Third-Level Domains](https://neil.fraser.name/news/2026/09/03/) ⭐️ 8.0/10

Verisign has decided to terminate all existing third-level registrations under the .name top-level domain, discontinuing domains of the form x.y.name while apparently releasing the underlying second-level domains y.name. This decision undermines the stability of the domain name system because existing registrants lose their online identifiers without fault, and it contradicts ICANN's mission to ensure stable, secure operation of unique identifier systems. It raises broader concerns about whether registry operators can unilaterally revoke leased domain names. The termination applies specifically to third-level registrations x.y.name, not to second-level .name domains such as dvt.name; there is no mention of temporarily reserving the released second-level domains to prevent squatting. This distinction is critical because many registrants may confuse the two tiers.

hackernews · pavel_lishin · Sep 3, 14:54 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49550772)

**Impact**: Immediately, owners of .name third-level domains—such as individuals using first.last.name for email or personal websites—must migrate to replacement domains, causing service disruptions and potential loss of identity. In the longer term, releasing y.name second-level domains invites domain squatting and phishing, while eroding trust in gTLD registries and strengthening arguments that domain names are only leased, not owned. The controversy may pressure ICANN to review registry termination policies and consumer protections.

**Background**: .name is a generic top-level domain (gTLD) originally intended for personal names, allowing both second-level registrations like john.name and third-level registrations like john.smith.name. In the DNS hierarchy, a third-level domain adds a label to the left of the second-level domain, and it is controlled by the same registry but leased to registrants. ICANN is the non-profit body that coordinates the DNS root and gTLD policies, with a mission to preserve operational stability and security of the Internet's unique identifier systems.
