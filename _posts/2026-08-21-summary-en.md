## [Accidental Hijack of e.164.arpa Logs Military Base Calls](https://lina.sh/blog/hijacking-e164-arpa) ⭐️ 8.0/10

The author accidentally took over the abandoned e.164.arpa ENUM infrastructure due to a long-standing misconfiguration and logged hundreds of thousands of phone calls directed to military bases. It demonstrates that obsolete but still routable telephony infrastructure can quietly expose call metadata, including highly sensitive military destinations, without anyone noticing. The incident highlights a neglected legacy protocol that has fallen through the cracks of telecom governance. ENUM in e164.arpa works by reversing E.164 phone numbers into DNS labels to resolve them to SIP URIs or other internet resources; the author’s setup logged the DNS queries rather than actual voice content. The incident appears to have been limited to metadata exposure, and the service remains routed despite being officially abandoned.

hackernews · gavide · Aug 21, 13:11 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49387570)

**Impact**: In the short term, the author gained access to hundreds of thousands of call routing queries involving military bases, revealing who was trying to reach those numbers. Long term, this may force operators and regulators to clean up or decommission legacy e.164.arpa/ENUM infrastructure and could lead to better monitoring of abandoned critical services.

**Background**: E.164 is the ITU-T numbering standard for international telephone numbers. ENUM maps those numbers into the Domain Name System under the reserved zone e164.arpa, enabling carriers to find IP-based endpoints for calls. Although e164.arpa was set aside for this purpose, the service saw little adoption and has been largely dead, yet some infrastructure still queries it.
