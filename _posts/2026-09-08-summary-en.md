## [OpenAI Claims Solution to Navier-Stokes Millennium Problem](https://www.reddit.com/r/MachineLearning/comments/1wavdi7/openal_says_it_has_cracked_one_of_maths/) ⭐️ 9.0/10

OpenAI announced in September 2026 that an internal AI system produced a proof showing that smooth, finite-energy three-dimensional incompressible Navier-Stokes flows can develop a singularity in finite time, thereby addressing one of the Clay Millennium Prize Problems. The proof was formalized in the Lean proof assistant and published on OpenAI's website. If verified, this would be only the second Millennium Prize Problem solved after the Poincaré conjecture and the first major mathematical breakthrough produced by AI at the frontier of pure mathematics. It would demonstrate that AI can contribute to deep theoretical research, not merely computation or pattern recognition. The proof reportedly establishes statements C and D of Fefferman's official problem formulation by showing finite-time singularity formation for smooth 3D incompressible Navier-Stokes flows with finite energy. It has been formalized in Lean but has not yet been independently verified, and the announcement is entangled in a priority dispute with two mathematicians who derived closely related Euler equation results.

reddit · r/MachineLearning · /u/Shizuka_Kuze · Sep 8, 17:42

**Impact**: The claim will now undergo independent verification by the mathematical community and the Clay Mathematics Institute; if confirmed, it could resolve a problem open for over two decades and trigger a wave of AI-assisted theorem proving across mathematics and physics. OpenAI said it would decline the $1 million prize if offered, shifting attention to scientific credit and research norms. The accompanying priority dispute with two mathematicians, including one at Anthropic, may also discourage open sharing of promising research directions.

**Background**: The Millennium Prize Problems are seven unsolved mathematical problems selected by the Clay Mathematics Institute in 2000, each with a $1 million prize. The Navier-Stokes existence and smoothness problem asks whether the equations governing fluid motion always have smooth, unique solutions in three dimensions, or whether singularities can form. As of 2026, the only officially solved Millennium Problem is the Poincaré conjecture.

This skill matters because it is a practical, reusable workaround that reflects broader demand for concise, ADHD-friendly agent communication. The skill is distributed as a markdown instruction set (SKILL.md) that can be placed in .cursor/skills or referenced via CLI; it mandates action-first and numbered steps, but users report the conciseness may fade after a few turns without repeated prompting.

**Background**: Coding agents like Claude Code and Cursor can be customized with 'skills'—reusable markdown instructions that are automatically applied to relevant tasks. ADHD-friendly formatting means putting the answer first, using numbered steps, and avoiding unnecessary qualifiers or digressions. Claude and similar LLMs are often criticized for verbose, hedging responses that bury the requested action.

## [NeurIPS desk-rejected 178 papers via flawed AI detector; track chairs' papers flagged](https://www.reddit.com/r/MachineLearning/comments/1wakf62/neurips_deskrejected_178_papers_for_being/) ⭐️ 8.0/10

NeurIPS's Position Paper Track used Pangram, a proprietary AI detector, to desk-reject 178 papers (18.4% of submissions) without human review or appeal. Independent tests found the same detector flagged the track chairs' own papers at 24–69%, and its default settings initially flagged 42.7% of all submissions. This matters because a top machine learning conference used an opaque, unreliable AI detector to make high-stakes acceptance decisions without human oversight. It highlights growing risks of automated AI policing in academia, especially its disproportionate false-positive rate for non-native English writers. The detector initially flagged 42.7% of submissions; organizers reduced text-window sizes to bring the desk-rejection rate down to 12.7%. Twenty-two papers were rejected because their Pangram score exceeded 0.5 despite authors denying AI use, and a Stanford study found 61.22% of human TOEFL essays can be falsely flagged.

reddit · r/MachineLearning · /u/tughanbulut · Sep 8, 10:19

**Impact**: The 178 rejected authors lose a NeurIPS position paper slot and must resubmit elsewhere (e.g., ICLR by Sept 25 or ICML), though the rejection is not recorded as misconduct. Conference organizers face pressure to revise or abandon automated AI screening, and Pangram's credibility may be damaged as academics examine its false positives. More broadly, researchers—especially non-native English speakers—may alter their writing style or avoid formal phrasing to evade false flags, potentially distorting scientific communication.

**Background**: NeurIPS (Conference on Neural Information Processing Systems) is one of the most prestigious annual machine learning conferences, with peer-reviewed papers and workshops. Pangram is an AI detection tool developed by Pangram Labs that uses natural language processing to identify text likely generated by large language models like ChatGPT or Claude. AI detectors have been criticized for unreliability and for enabling "witch hunts" against authors. Position Paper tracks are a submission category focused on forward-looking ideas rather than completed experiments.
